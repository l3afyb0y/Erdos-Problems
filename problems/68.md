# Erdős Problem 68

**Database status checked 2026-08-30:** OPEN.

## Problem

Is

\[
S=\sum_{n\ge 2}\frac1{n!-1}
\]

irrational?

## Candidate partial lemma: a prime-by-prime p-adic cancellation obstruction

Fix an odd prime `p`. Define

\[
R_p=\{n:2\le n\le p-2,\ n!\equiv1\pmod p\}.
\]

This set is never empty: Wilson's theorem gives `(p-2)! \equiv 1 (mod p)`.

For `n in R_p`, write

\[
e_p(n)=v_p(n!-1),\qquad
u_p(n)=\frac{n!-1}{p^{e_p(n)}}\pmod p,
\]

where `nu_p(n)` is nonzero modulo `p`. Put

\[
E_p=\max_{n\in R_p} e_p(n).
\]

### Lemma

If `S` is rational, then for every sufficiently large prime `p`,

\[
\boxed{\sum_{\substack{n\in R_p\\ e_p(n)=E_p}}\nu_p(n)^{-1}\equiv0\pmod p.}
\]

Here the inverses are taken modulo `p`.

### Proof

Assume `S=A/B` in lowest terms and choose a prime `p` not dividing `B`.

For every `n>=p`, the factorial `n!` is divisible by `p`, so

\[
n!-1\equiv-1\pmod p.
\]

Thus every term of the infinite tail beginning at `n=p` is `p`-adically integral. Among the finitely many preceding terms, a negative `p`-adic valuation occurs exactly when `n in R_p`.

Separate the singular finite sum

\[
T_p=\sum_{n\in R_p}\frac1{n!-1}.
\]

All terms in `S-T_p` are `p`-adically integral. Since `S=A/B` is also `p`-adically integral, `T_p` must be `p`-adically integral.

Multiply `T_p` by `p^{E_p}`. Terms with `e_p(n)<E_p` become divisible by `p`; terms with `e_p(n)=E_p` reduce modulo `p` to `nu_p(n)^{-1}`. Integrality therefore forces

\[
\sum_{e_p(n)=E_p}\nu_p(n)^{-1}\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

This proves the lemma.

## Wilson-quotient specialization

Let

\[
W_p=\frac{(p-1)!+1}{p}
\]

be the Wilson quotient. Modulo `p^2`,

\[
(p-2)!\equiv 1+p(1-W_p)\pmod{p^2}.
\]

Hence, whenever `v_p((p-2)!-1)=1`, the unit attached to the forced terminal root is

\[
\nu_p(p-2)\equiv1-W_p\pmod p.
\]

So the unavoidable Wilson root contributes an explicit summand to the cancellation condition.

## Exact computation

A fresh exact modular scan was run for every prime

\[
5\le p<20000.
\]

There are `2260` such primes. Factorials were iterated modulo `p^3`, all indices in `R_p` were found, their `p`-adic depths through the maximal occurring depth were determined, and the boxed congruence was evaluated exactly.

The rationality-required congruence **fails for 2258 of the 2260 primes**. The only cancellations in this range are:

- `p=139`, with simple roots `n=69,122,137`;
- `p=2593`, with simple roots `n=349,2243,2591`.

No prime below `20000` required precision beyond `p^3` to determine the maximal-depth obstruction.

This is finite evidence only. Rationality would be contradicted if one could prove that the boxed congruence fails for infinitely many primes.

## Why the naive private-prime argument fails

Every prime divisor `p` of some `n!-1` satisfies `p>n`. One might hope such a prime is unique to that denominator. It never is globally unique: Wilson's theorem always gives

\[
p\mid (p-2)!-1.
\]

Thus the correct obstruction is not uniqueness of a prime divisor but failure of the precise deepest-level `p`-adic cancellation above.

## Prior-art audit

The maintained Erdős Problems page still lists #68 as OPEN. Searches targeted at the displayed series together with `p`-adic cancellation, Wilson quotients, and factorial residues did not locate this exact obstruction. Work on factorial residues modulo primes is substantial, so this should be treated as a **candidate partial lemma**, not as a settled novelty claim without expert literature review.

## Classification

**candidate/new partial lemma** — exact necessary `p`-adic cancellation criterion plus finite verification through all primes below `20000`; no complete irrationality proof.
