# Erdős Problem #172

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30)

**Final classification:** useful reformulation / diagonal-vs-off-diagonal Ramsey obstruction

## Statement

In every finite colouring of the positive integers, do there exist arbitrarily large finite sets `A` such that all sums and products of distinct elements of `A` have one common colour?

Current status source: https://www.erdosproblems.com/172

## Exact Ramsey reduction

Fix a finite colouring `c:N->C`. Colour each unordered pair `{x,y}` by the ordered pair

`lambda(x,y)=(c(x+y), c(xy))`.

This is a finite edge-colouring of the complete graph on `N`. Infinite Ramsey therefore gives an infinite set `B` and colours `sigma,pi` such that for every distinct `x,y in B`,

`c(x+y)=sigma` and `c(xy)=pi`.

Thus arbitrary finite colourings always admit an infinite set on which **all pairwise sums have one colour and all pairwise products have one colour**. The whole remaining difficulty of Erdős #172 is exactly the diagonal condition

`sigma=pi`.

Equivalently, a counterexample colouring would have to force every infinite Ramsey-homogeneous set for the pair-labeling above to stabilize on an off-diagonal label `(sigma,pi)` with `sigma!=pi`.

This cleanly separates the ordinary Ramsey component from the genuinely arithmetic sum-product compatibility component.

## Attempts past the reduction

Scaling `B` by a common factor does not solve the mismatch because sums scale by `t` while products scale by `t^2`, and an arbitrary finite colouring need not respect either action. Translation is even less compatible with products. No invariant was found forcing an off-diagonal homogeneous set to generate a diagonal one.

Moreira's theorem supplies the first nontrivial two-element diagonal configuration; Alweiss proves the full finite-size statement over nonzero rationals, but neither presently closes this integer synchronization step.

## Prior-art audit

A July 2026 public report gives a stronger finite weak-hypergraph formulation and large exact finite computations. The infinite-Ramsey pair-label reduction above is therefore recorded only as a useful structural reformulation, not as a novelty claim.

## Bottom line

Problem #172 remains open. The exact missing statement is not Ramsey homogeneity itself, but equality of the two stabilized operation-colours.
