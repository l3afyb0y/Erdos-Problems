## Statement

For every integer `n > 2`, do there exist distinct positive integers `x < y < z` such that

`4/n = 1/x + 1/y + 1/z`?

## Elementary identities recovered in the attempt

Several residue classes admit immediate decompositions.

### Even `n`

Write `n = 2m`. Then

`4/n = 1/m + 1/(m+1) + 1/(m(m+1))`.

For `n > 2`, the three denominators are distinct and increasing.

### `n = 3m - 1`

Then

`4/n = 1/m + 1/n + 1/(mn)`.

### `n = 4m - 1`

Let `t = mn`. Then

`4/n = 1/m + 1/(t+1) + 1/(t(t+1))`.

### `n ≡ 13 (mod 24)`

Let

`x = (n+3)/4`,  `Q = nx`.

Here `x` is even and

`4/n - 1/x = 3/Q = 1/(Q/2) + 1/Q`.

Hence

`4/n = 1/x + 1/(Q/2) + 1/Q`.

## Composite reduction

If `d | n` and

`4/d = 1/a + 1/b + 1/c`,

then multiplying all three denominators by `n/d` gives a solution for `n`:

`4/n = 1/((n/d)a) + 1/((n/d)b) + 1/((n/d)c)`.

Thus, after the elementary residue identities and induction through proper divisors, it is enough to focus on primes. For primes greater than `3`, the identities above remove every residue class modulo `24` except

`p ≡ 1 (mod 24)`.

This matches the standard hard residue class.

## Hard-class factorization

For `n ≡ 1 (mod 24)`, choose a positive integer step `k` and set

`a = 4k - 1`,

`x = (n+a)/4`,

`Q = nx`.

Then

`4/n - 1/x = a/Q`.

To split the residual term into two unit fractions,

`a/Q = 1/y + 1/z`,

is equivalent to

`(ay - Q)(az - Q) = Q^2`.

Therefore a factor pair `D E = Q^2` gives a solution whenever

`D ≡ -Q (mod a)` and `E ≡ -Q (mod a)`,

with

`y = (D+Q)/a`,  `z = (E+Q)/a`.

If `gcd(a,Q)=1`, finding one divisor `D | Q^2` with `D ≡ -Q (mod a)` automatically forces the complementary divisor `E = Q^2/D` into the same congruence class.

## Exact finite evidence from the experiment

The search was implemented exactly and run over every prime `p < 1,000,000` with `p ≡ 1 (mod 24)`. There are `9,732` such primes in the tested range. Every one admitted a witness with `k <= 15` in the factorization search above.

Observed first-witness depths:

`{1:5192, 2:3551, 3:594, 4:129, 5:132, 6:81, 7:13, 8:28, 9:1, 10:3, 11:2, 12:2, 13:1, 14:1, 15:2}`.

Examples at maximal tested depth included `118801` and `806521`.

This is finite evidence only. It does not establish a universal bound on `k`, and no such extrapolation is claimed.

## Missing step

A complete proof still requires a universal argument that for every prime `p ≡ 1 (mod 24)`, some step `k` and divisor of `Q^2` satisfy the required congruence. The finite sweep does not provide that quantifier bridge.
