# Erdős Problem 130

**Database status checked 2026-08-30:** OPEN in `teorth/erdosproblems` (`data/problems.yaml`, current main).

**Experiment classification:** useful reformulation / elementary structural lemma.

## Problem

Let `A subset R^2` be infinite, with no three points collinear and no four points concyclic. Form a graph on `A` by joining two points exactly when their Euclidean distance is a positive integer. How large can its chromatic and clique numbers be? In particular, can its chromatic number be infinite?

## Integer-shell lemma

Fix a vertex `v`. For any positive integer `m`, every neighbor of `v` at distance exactly `m` lies on the circle centered at `v` with radius `m`.

Because the point set contains no four concyclic points, that circle contains at most three points of `A`. Therefore:

**For every vertex and every positive integer radius, there are at most three neighbors at that radius.**

Consequently, if `F` is a finite induced subgraph whose Euclidean diameter is at most `D`, then every vertex has degree at most

`3 floor(D)`.

Hence greedy coloring gives

`chi(F) <= 3 floor(D) + 1`.

A slightly more local form is useful. Every finite graph of chromatic number `t` contains a `t`-critical subgraph and therefore a subgraph of minimum degree at least `t-1`. Applying the shell bound at each vertex shows:

**Any finite `t`-chromatic integer-distance configuration in this strong general position must, from every vertex of a critical core, contain an integer-distance edge of length at least `ceil((t-1)/3)`.**

## Prose version

Stand at one point and sort its integer-distance neighbors by distance. Distance one is one circle, distance two is another circle, and so on. The geometry forbids four points from sharing any circle, so each integer shell can hold at most three neighbors. High chromatic complexity therefore cannot be packed into only short integer distances: a critical high-chromatic finite piece is forced to use longer and longer integer edges.

This does not settle the problem, because an infinite configuration may spread over arbitrarily large scales. It does turn `infinite chromatic number` into a quantitative multiscale requirement rather than an undifferentiated coloring question.

## Status / novelty note

The shell bound is elementary and no novelty claim is made. Its role here is to expose a clean local obstruction that follows immediately from the `no four concyclic` hypothesis and that can be combined with stronger geometric information in later passes.
