# Erdős Problem #208

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN  
**Status checked:** 2026-08-30  
**Experiment result:** useful reformulation / exact squarefree-kernel injection; no frontier progress

## Problem

Let

\[
s_1<s_2<\cdots
\]

be the squarefree positive integers. Is it true that, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),

\[
s_{n+1}-s_n\ll_\varepsilon s_n^\varepsilon
\]

for all sufficiently large \(n\)? Is the sharper bound

\[
s_{n+1}-s_n\le (1+o(1))\frac{\pi^2}{6}\frac{\log s_n}{\log\log s_n}
\]

true?

Maintained source: https://www.erdosproblems.com/208

The maintained database still labels the problem OPEN. A narrow pre-discovery audit found no credible complete proof or disproof. The best current unconditional result located in the post-discovery audit is Pandey's theorem that every sufficiently large interval of length \(X^{1/5-\eta}\) contains a squarefree integer for some absolute \(\eta>0\); an explicit/formalized 2026 version gives \(\eta=2/90935\) up to an arbitrary positive epsilon in the exponent.

## Discovery record

The unresolved quantifier is global: every sufficiently large gap must be smaller than every fixed positive power of its location. A finite computation cannot discharge this.

Let \(X,H\ge1\) be integers and suppose that every integer

\[
X+1,\ldots,X+H
\]

is non-squarefree. The useful state distinction is whether a position is already hit by the square of a small prime or must obtain its square divisor from a large prime.

### Exact kernel-injection lemma

Let \(y>H/2\). Write \(Q(T)\) for the number of squarefree positive integers at most \(T\). Then

\[
\boxed{
H\le
\sum_{p\le y}
\left(
\left\lfloor\frac{X+H}{p^2}\right\rfloor-
\left\lfloor\frac{X}{p^2}\right\rfloor
\right)
+Q\!\left(\frac{X+H}{y^2}\right).
}
\]

#### Proof

Call a position \(i\in\{1,\ldots,H\}\) **small-hit** if \(p^2\mid X+i\) for some prime \(p\le y\). The number of small-hit positions is at most the displayed sum over primes, by a union bound.

Now take a position which is not small-hit. Write its unique squarefree-kernel decomposition as

\[
X+i=a_i d_i^2,
\]

where \(a_i\) is squarefree. Because \(X+i\) is non-squarefree, \(d_i>1\). Because no prime \(p\le y\) has \(p^2\mid X+i\), every prime factor of \(d_i\) exceeds \(y\), hence

\[
d_i>y.
\]

The map from such positions to their squarefree kernels \(a_i\) is injective. Indeed, if two distinct positions \(i,j\) had the same kernel \(a\), then

\[
|i-j|=a|d_i^2-d_j^2|.
\]

The roots \(d_i,d_j\) are distinct integers exceeding \(y\), so

\[
|d_i^2-d_j^2|\ge d_i+d_j>2y>H,
\]

which contradicts \(|i-j|<H\).

Finally,

\[
a_i=\frac{X+i}{d_i^2}<\frac{X+H}{y^2}.
\]

Thus the residual positions inject into the squarefree integers below \((X+H)/y^2\), proving the inequality.

### Coarse asymptotic consequence

Using

\[
\sum_p\frac1{p^2}=P(2)=0.4522474\ldots,
\qquad
Q(T)\sim\frac6{\pi^2}T,
\qquad
\pi(y)\sim\frac{y}{\log y},
\]

and the elementary estimate

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{X+H}{p^2}\right\rfloor-
\left\lfloor\frac{X}{p^2}\right\rfloor
\le \frac{H}{p^2}+1,
\]

choose

\[
y=\lambda H\log H
\]

with \(0<\lambda<1-P(2)\). The exact lemma then gives

\[
\bigl(1-P(2)-\lambda+o(1)\bigr)H
\le
\left(\frac6{\pi^2}+o(1)\right)
\frac{X}{\lambda^2H^2(\log H)^2}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
X\ge
\left(
\frac{\lambda^2(1-P(2)-\lambda)}{6/\pi^2}
+o(1)
\right)
H^3(\log H)^2.
\]

The coefficient is maximized at

\[
\lambda=\frac23(1-P(2)),
\]

which gives

\[
X\ge
\left(
\frac{4(1-P(2))^3}{27(6/\pi^2)}+o(1)
\right)
H^3(\log H)^2.
\]

Numerically the constant is about \(0.04005\). In particular this route yields the much weaker scale

\[
H=O\!\left(\frac{X^{1/3}}{(\log X)^{2/3}}\right).
\]

The exact injection is the useful structural statement; this asymptotic corollary is included only to show quantitatively where the route stops.

## What the lemma enables, and why it still stalls

The lemma converts every residual position into a **distinct smaller squarefree kernel**. This is stronger than merely assigning a distinct large prime square: it preserves the maximal square-part state and turns a gap of length \(H\) into an injection into squarefree integers of scale roughly \(X/y^2\).

Trying to iterate the map does not reproduce the original problem, however: the kernels are squarefree but are not consecutive and need not themselves form a gap. The recursion therefore loses the local interval structure required for another application of the same lemma.

Equivalently, after dyadically grouping the large square roots \(d\), one reaches the modern lattice-point object

\[
X<ad^2\le X+H,
\]

or, in fractional-part form, a count of \(d\) for which \(X/d^2\) lies unusually close to an integer. That is precisely the large-square short-interval bottleneck on which the current literature uses substantially deeper lattice-point/equidistribution machinery.

So the unresolved quantifier has not moved to \(X^{o(1)}\): the argument remains polynomial-scale.

## Verification

The proof above is exact and does not depend on computation. The accompanying checker [`verification/208_kernel_injection.py`](../verification/208_kernel_injection.py) independently enumerates all maximal non-squarefree runs ending below 50,000 and checks the displayed exact inequality for several boundary/adaptive choices of \(y>H/2\). It finds no counterexample. This finite test is adversarial support only, not evidence for the unbounded conjecture.

## Prior-art audit

Post-discovery searching found that the decisive large-square representation is firmly inside the known architecture of the problem:

- Filaseta and Trifonov proved a squarefree occurs in intervals of length \(cX^{1/5}\log X\) for all large \(X\).
- Pandey improved the exponent to \(1/5-\eta\) for some absolute \(\eta>0\), specifically by obtaining stronger counts for integers in a short interval divisible by a large square.
- Pandey's August 2026 explicit/formalized version records an explicit exponent \(1/5-2/90935+\varepsilon\) in its stated range and formalizes the large-square counting argument.
- Public descriptions of that proof formulate the critical count through integers \(d\) with \(X/d^2\) very near an integer, exactly the lattice-point representation reached when the kernel-injection lemma is pushed further.

Sources:

- https://www.erdosproblems.com/208
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13981
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06682

I did not locate the exact boxed kernel-injection inequality stated in this form, but it is far weaker than established results and its operative large-square reduction is standard prior architecture. Search failure is not evidence of novelty. It is therefore recorded conservatively as a **useful reformulation**, not a candidate new lemma.
