# Erdős Problem 33

**Current status checked 2026-08-30:** OPEN.

**Experiment status:** useful reformulation / no new bound.

## Statement

Let `A subset N` be an additive complement to the squares: every sufficiently large integer is `m^2+a` for some `a in A`. Determine the smallest possible `limsup A(N)/sqrt(N)`; in particular, must `liminf A(N)/sqrt(N)>1`?

## Exact finite coverage inequality

For every sufficiently large `N`, covering all integers up to `N` gives

`N-O(1) <= sum_{a in A, a<=N} floor(sqrt(N-a)) + O(A(N))`,

with the harmless endpoint convention depending on whether `0^2` is allowed.

Equivalently, each shift `a` covers exactly the targets obtained by adding squares not exceeding `N-a`; its coverage capacity decays as `a` approaches the boundary `N`.

The trivial estimate `floor(sqrt(N-a))<=sqrt(N)` immediately yields

`A(N) >= (1-o(1))sqrt(N)`.

So the constant `1` is the pure counting barrier. Improving it requires exploiting the fact that a single fixed sparse set `A` must cover **every scale simultaneously**: one cannot place all shifts near the most efficient part of every interval at once.

## Continuous kernel exposed by lexicalization

At the heuristic density level, squares contribute a kernel proportional to `1/(2 sqrt(t))`. If `A(x)` had local growth like `C sqrt(x)`, then its formal derivative would contribute `C/(2 sqrt(x))`. Their additive convolution has constant mass

`(C/4) * integral_0^1 du / sqrt(u(1-u)) = C*pi/4`.

Demanding average representation mass at least one suggests the threshold `C>=4/pi`. This exactly matches the best lower constant recorded on the maintained problem page.

The experiment therefore independently exposed why `pi` and the value `4/pi` are natural: they arise from the beta-integral coupling the two square-root boundary singularities. However, turning this heuristic into the known rigorous liminf theorem requires the global density-control argument from the literature; no new proof or improvement was obtained here.

## Prior-art audit

The maintained page lists #33 as OPEN and records the best-known lower bound `liminf A(N)/sqrt(N) >= 4/pi`, due independently to Cilleruelo, Habsieger, and Balasubramanian--Ramana, together with a current explicit limsup construction below about `6.66`.

Thus the `4/pi` kernel observation is a known frontier explanation, not novel progress.
