# Erdős Problem #213

**Maintained status at attempt:** OPEN  
**Attempt date:** 2026-08-30  
**Final classification:** useful computational obstruction / tool-assisted gluing search; no complete progress

## Problem

For every integer `n >= 4`, do there exist `n` points in the Euclidean plane such that all pairwise distances are integers, no three points are collinear, and no four points are concyclic?

The maintained Erdős Problems page still lists the problem as open and records the Kreisel–Kurz seven-point construction as the best known unconditional example.

## Unresolved quantifier

The target is **for every n**. Producing or excluding one particular construction mechanism for `n=8` does not settle the problem. Even a genuine eight-point example would only move the known finite frontier by one unless accompanied by a closure operation producing arbitrarily large examples.

## Discovery path

The first language-first branch searched for a closure operation. Rational distances are sufficient for any fixed finite configuration because multiplying by a common denominator makes all distances integral.

Several abundant rational-distance families lie on a line or a circle. Similarities and inversions preserve rational distances under suitable rationality hypotheses, but they also preserve generalized-circle incidence: lines and circles map to lines and circles. Thus this route cannot convert the easy collinear/cyclic families into sets satisfying both general-position restrictions.

The next branch asked whether the two published Kreisel–Kurz heptagons could be composed.

Both published distance matrices have characteristic `D = 2002`. After fixing an edge, either heptagon has an exact embedding in coordinates

`(x, sqrt(D) y)` with `x,y in Q`.

If an edge of one configuration is identified with an edge of the other by a similarity, every cross squared distance is therefore rational. The remaining condition for a cross distance to be rational is simply that this rational squared distance be a rational square. This converts a geometric gluing attempt into a finite exact compatibility problem.

During exploratory computation, one edge alignment initially appeared to give eight labels at pairwise rational distance. Preserving geometric state exposed the error: the apparent eighth point was exactly the third vertex of a congruent shared triangle, so the two labels represented the same point. This motivated an exact verifier that merges coincident coordinates before counting.

## Exact edge-gluing obstruction

Let `H1` and `H2` be the two seven-point distance matrices published by Kreisel and Kurz (2008). For each of the

`21 * 21 * 2 = 882`

choices consisting of an undirected edge of `H1`, an undirected edge of `H2`, and one of the two orientations (direct/reflected), do the following:

1. embed both heptagons exactly in `Q(sqrt(2002))^2`;
2. map the chosen edge of `H2` to the chosen edge of `H1` by the unique corresponding similarity;
3. merge geometrically coincident points;
4. build the exact rational-distance compatibility relation using `Fraction` arithmetic and rational-square testing;
5. exhaustively inspect all subsets of the resulting union;
6. reject a subset if any pair has irrational distance, any triple is collinear, or any quadruple is concyclic.

The exhaustive result is

`maximum admissible union subset = 7`.

Therefore **no configuration obtained by gluing the two published Kreisel–Kurz heptagons along an edge, with arbitrary scale forced by that edge and either orientation, contains an eight-point rational-distance subset in general position.**

This is only an obstruction to this finite composition mechanism. It does not imply that an eight-point configuration does not exist.

## Tool jurisdiction

- **Selection/status:** web search was used only to confirm that #213 is currently marked OPEN and that seven points remain the maintained frontier.
- **Primary discovery:** language-first reasoning identified inversion/generalized-circle preservation and then the possibility of composing same-characteristic heptagons.
- **Tool-assisted discovery:** exact Python exploration materially discovered the near-gluing pattern and the coincident-point failure, and then motivated the exhaustive edge-gluing formulation. This result must therefore be described as computational/tool-assisted discovery, not prose-only discovery.
- **Verification:** a separately written exact `Fraction`-arithmetic checker exhaustively reran all 882 edge alignments and confirmed maximum admissible cardinality 7.
- **Prior art:** post-discovery searches found the standard characteristic machinery, the two 2008 integral heptagons, later work constructing many 7-clusters, and current sources still stating that the eight-point case is unknown. No source located in this audit states this exact two-heptagon edge-gluing obstruction. Search failure is not a novelty proof.

## Why the branch does not close

The gluing mechanism has no surviving recursion. Identifying an edge fixes the relative similarity completely; the exact compatibility sweep shows that neither of the two known heptagons can contribute enough genuinely new mutually compatible vertices to cross from seven to eight. The result says nothing about:

- other heptagons (later work reports many 7-clusters);
- gluings not based on a shared edge;
- configurations not assembled from known seven-point sets;
- an abstract closure operation capable of producing arbitrarily large sets.

Hence the original universal quantifier has not moved.

## Prior-art notes

Kreisel and Kurz (2008) give two integral heptagons in general position and prove the minimum diameter for seven points. Their paper also establishes/uses the common characteristic of all nondegenerate triangles in a planar integral point set. Later work (`Constructing 7-clusters`, 2014) reports more than 1000 seven-clusters, so the present finite obstruction should not be read as a general obstruction to composing arbitrary heptagons.

Current maintained and formalization-facing sources still describe seven as the best known size and eight as unknown as of the 2026-08-30 audit.
