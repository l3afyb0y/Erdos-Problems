# Erdős Problem #9

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN  
**Experiment result:** useful reformulation / exact negative finite search; no complete progress

## Problem

Let `A` be the odd positive integers which are not of the form

\[
n=p+2^k+2^\ell
\]

with `p` prime and `k,l>=0`. Must `A` have positive upper density?

## Exact finite-cover criterion

A particularly strong sufficient condition for a positive-density exceptional set would be the following. Choose finitely many odd primes `q`, residues `r_q (mod q)`, and a common exponent period `L` divisible by every `ord_q(2)`. If for every pair `(i,j) in (Z/LZ)^2` there is some chosen `q` for which

\[
2^i+2^j\equiv r_q\pmod q,
\]

then CRT gives an arithmetic progression `n ≡ r_q (mod q)` simultaneously for all chosen `q`. Apart from finitely many exceptional equalities where the prime summand itself equals the witnessing divisor `q`, every sufficiently large odd integer in that progression is not `p+2^k+2^l`. Thus such a certificate would imply positive lower density, hence solve the problem affirmatively.

This is an exact reduction for the special strategy of producing one fixed exceptional arithmetic progression.

## Exact search

The verifier formulates the certificate search as a 0-1 set-cover/MILP problem: for each prime `q` with `ord_q(2)|L`, at most one residue `r_q` may be chosen, and all unordered exponent pairs modulo `L` must be covered.

Exact searches were run for

`L = 12, 24, 36, 60, 72, 84`.

Every instance was certified infeasible by HiGHS. Thus no certificate of this restricted periodic type exists for any of these periods using all primes whose order of `2` divides the period.

## Prior art

The maintained literature notes that Erdős believed covering systems could not prove the result, in the stronger sense that representable integers should occur in every infinite arithmetic progression. Crocker proved `>> log log N` exceptions and Pan proved `>>_eps N^(1-eps)` exceptions. Therefore the finite-cover criterion is best regarded as a useful computational formulation of a known strategic direction, not a new theorem.
