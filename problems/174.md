# Erdős Problem #174

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30)

**Final classification:** no progress / affine-dependence frontier after recent pyramid closure

## Statement

Characterise the finite Euclidean Ramsey sets: a finite `A subset R^n` is Ramsey if for every finite number of colours there is a dimension `d` such that every colouring of `R^d` contains a monochromatic congruent copy of `A`.

Current status source: https://www.erdosproblems.com/174

## Discovery attempt

The language-first representation is: a configuration is Ramsey when increasing ambient dimension eventually makes its complete finite distance pattern unavoidable in one colour. The known necessary condition says all points must lie on one sphere. Thus the unresolved direction asks whether a common-radius representation alone forces monochromatic recurrence of the entire distance matrix.

I tried to use closure under adjoining points to grow an arbitrary spherical configuration from a smaller Ramsey seed. The decisive distinction is whether the new point lies outside or inside the affine hull of what has already been built.

If every new point lies outside the current affine hull, the construction can only increase affine dimension at every step. Starting from one point, this produces affinely independent configurations (simplices). It cannot directly build an arbitrary spherical configuration containing affine dependencies. The recent pyramid theorem of Kenneth Moore proves exactly the outside-affine-hull closure for an arbitrary Ramsey base, so this attempted induction stops precisely at the inside-affine-hull case.

I also rewrote a centred spherical configuration by its Gram matrix. A transitive-envelope proof would amount to realising that Gram matrix as a principal submatrix of a finite spherical configuration on which a finite isometry group acts transitively. This is the subtransitivity route already present in the literature, not a new closure theorem.

## Current-claim / prior-art audit

The live database remains OPEN. A 10 August 2026 preprint by Kenneth Moore proves that adjoining a point outside the affine hull of a finite Ramsey base preserves Ramsey-ness. A current audit reports this as a substantial partial result, not a complete characterization. It does not settle the inside-affine-hull step, spherical implies Ramsey, or spherical implies subtransitive.

## Bottom line

No complete characterization or new partial lemma was obtained. The strongest branch collapses onto the known current frontier: off-affine-hull extension is now available, while affine dependence remains the obstruction to iterating that closure into the general spherical case.
