# Erdős Problem #170

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30)

**Final classification:** no progress / duplicate-difference excess barrier

## Statement

Let `F(N)` be the least size of `A subset {0,...,N}` such that every `0<=d<=N` occurs as a difference of two elements of `A`. Determine `lim F(N)/sqrt(N)`.

Current status source: https://www.erdosproblems.com/170

## Discovery pass

If `m=|A|`, let `r(d)` be the number of pairs `a>b` in `A` with `a-b=d`. Completeness says `r(d)>=1` for every `1<=d<=N`, while

`sum_{d=1}^N r(d)=binom(m,2)`.

Hence the exact duplicate-difference excess is

`E(A)=sum_{d=1}^N (r(d)-1)=binom(m,2)-N`.

The elementary square-root lower bound is just nonnegativity of this excess. Any improvement of the limiting constant must therefore prove that a complete ruler is forced to spend a positive proportion of its pair budget on repeated differences.

The endpoint condition is also exact: because the distance `N` must occur and all marks lie in `[0,N]`, both `0` and `N` belong to every admissible ruler. Reflection `A -> N-A` preserves all representation counts, so any structural argument can be symmetrized at the level of difference multiplicities.

I tried to force a uniform lower bound on `E(A)` from endpoint pairs, complementary marks, and additive-energy inequalities. These routes do not beat the classical pair-capacity bound without extra structure; sparse sets of size `Theta(sqrt N)` need not contain enough complementary marks, and additive energy admits too much concentration.

## Prior art

The maintained record gives the stronger classical lower constant about `1.56` (Leech) and upper constant `sqrt(3)` (Wichmann), so the identities above do not improve the frontier.

## Bottom line

No new quantitative progress. The useful diagnostic is that any elementary improvement must certify unavoidable repeated-difference mass beyond the mere `binom(m,2)>=N` count.
