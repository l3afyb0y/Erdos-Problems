# Erdős Problem #160

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30)

**Final classification:** known reduction independently rediscovered / current upper frontier updated

## Statement

Let `h(N)` be the smallest number of colours needed to colour `[N]` so that every nontrivial four-term arithmetic progression uses at least three distinct colours. Estimate `h(N)`.

Current status source: https://www.erdosproblems.com/160

## Exact pair-of-colours reformulation

Let the colour classes be `C_1,...,C_k`. The required condition is equivalent to:

> for every pair of colours `i<j`, the union `C_i union C_j` is 4-AP-free.

The forward implication is immediate: a 4-AP inside `C_i union C_j` would use at most two colours. Conversely, any 4-AP using at most two colours is contained in the union of those two colour classes.

If `r_4(N)` denotes the largest size of a 4-AP-free subset of `[N]`, then for every `i<j`,

\[
|C_i|+|C_j|\le r_4(N).
\]

Summing over all `binom(k,2)` pairs, each colour class is counted exactly `k-1` times, so

\[
(k-1)N\le \binom{k}{2}r_4(N).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{h(N)\ge \frac{2N}{r_4(N)}}.
\]

This is exact as a reduction, but it is weaker than the currently quoted lower route using 3-AP-free sets in an initial segment.

## Current upper-bound audit

The maintained Erdős Problems page still displays the older upper exponent `log(3)/log(22) approx 0.355`. A July 22, 2026 preprint by Ruizhe Shi and Yiqi Dong gives a stronger construction and states

\[
\boxed{h(N)\le N^{1/4+o(1)}}.
\]

Source: arXiv:2607.20752, *An Improved Upper Bound for Colorings Without Symmetrically Colored k-Term Arithmetic Progressions*.

This does not solve the order-of-growth problem, but it is the relevant current frontier and supersedes the older exponent recorded on the database page.

## Discovery attempt and barrier

The pair-union condition turns the colouring into a family of classes whose every two-class union avoids one fixed additive configuration. This is a strong global compatibility constraint, but the straightforward averaging inequality loses all information about overlap structure between *different* pairs of colour classes. I did not obtain a recursive/product obstruction stronger than the known quantitative 3-AP lower-bound route, nor a construction improving the July 2026 `1/4+o(1)` exponent.

A public August 2026 candidate write-up already records essentially the same pair-of-colours equivalence, so it is classified here as a known reduction independently rediscovered rather than novel progress.

## Bottom line

No complete solution. The useful run output is the exact two-colour-union equivalence plus a current-status correction: the public upper frontier is now `N^(1/4+o(1))`, not the older `N^(0.355+o(1))` displayed by the maintained page at the time of this run.
