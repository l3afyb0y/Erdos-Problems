# Erdős Problem 414

**Database status checked 2026-08-30:** OPEN.

**Experiment classification:** known structural reductions independently rediscovered / no new progress.

## Problem

Let `h(n)=n+tau(n)`, where `tau(n)` is the divisor-counting function, and iterate `h`. Must the forward orbits of every two positive integers eventually meet?

## Structural observations

The map is strictly increasing. Since `tau(n)` is odd exactly when `n` is a square,

`h(n) == n (mod 2)` for nonsquares,

while a square flips parity. Thus squares are the only parity gates in the dynamics.

Also

`tau(n) <= 2 sqrt(n)`,

so every step from `n` lies in

`(n, n+2 sqrt(n)]`.

Equivalently, any predecessor of `m` must lie in a short backward window of length `O(sqrt(m))`. This turns global coalescence into a synchronization problem for two increasing paths with sublinear step size, with parity changes possible only at square inputs.

The natural attempted route was to show that two noncoalescing paths would eventually acquire incompatible parity while both avoid square gates, or else would be forced into the same short predecessor window. Neither implication could be made universal: paths can interlace with sublinear jumps, and the square-gate condition alone does not force a collision.

## Prior-art audit

A June 2026 preprint by Eric Li, *Square-Annular Dynamics and Coalescence Frontiers for n+tau(n)*, already develops substantially stronger square/frontier reductions and explicitly leaves the synchronization step open. A July 2026 exact computation independently certified coalescence for all starting pairs up to `10^10`.

Accordingly, the observations above are not new progress.
