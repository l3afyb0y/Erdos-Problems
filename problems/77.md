# Erdős Problem #77

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30)

## Problem

For the diagonal Ramsey number `R(k)`, determine the value of `lim_{k->infinity} R(k)^(1/k)`.

## Result

**Final classification:** no progress / approximate-submultiplicativity barrier.

The language-first attack asks what would force existence of the exponential growth constant. A sufficient route would be an inequality making `log R(k)` subadditive up to `o(k)`: for example

`log R(k+l) <= log R(k)+log R(l)+o(k+l)`

uniformly in comparable `k,l`, after which a Fekete-type argument would yield the limit.

Standard Ramsey recurrences naturally compare diagonal numbers to off-diagonal Ramsey numbers and introduce an exponential entropy cost depending on the split. I found no way to make that cost sublinear, and therefore no route from the classical recurrences to the desired approximate subadditivity.

No new bound on `liminf` or `limsup` was obtained.
