# Erdős Problem 60

**Database status checked 2026-08-30:** OPEN.

## Problem

Must every graph on `n` vertices with more than `ex(n,C_4)` edges contain at least a constant multiple of `sqrt(n)` copies of `C_4`?

## Exact codegree identity

For distinct vertices `u,v`, let

\[
c_{uv}=|N(u)\cap N(v)|.
\]

Every 4-cycle has exactly two opposite vertex pairs, so the exact number `t` of copies of `C_4` is

\[
\boxed{
 t=\frac12\sum_{u<v}\binom{c_{uv}}2.
}
\]

Also

\[
\sum_{u<v}c_{uv}=\sum_v\binom{d(v)}2,
\]

because both sides count length-two paths by their endpoint pair or their middle vertex.

Since `(c-1)_+ <= binom(c,2)` for every nonnegative integer `c`,

\[
\sum_{u<v}c_{uv}
\le \binom n2+2t.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
 t\ge \frac12\left(\sum_v\binom{d(v)}2-\binom n2\right).
}
\]

By convexity of `x -> binom(x,2)` and `sum_v d(v)=2m`,

\[
\sum_v\binom{d(v)}2\ge \frac{2m^2}{n}-m,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
 t\ge \max\left\{0,\frac{m^2}{n}-\frac m2-\frac{n(n-1)}4\right\}.
}
\]

This is the classical codegree/Reiman-type supersaturation estimate. It becomes useful once `m` is sufficiently above the analytic `C_4` extremal threshold, but it need not be positive merely from `m=ex(n,C_4)+1`.

## Near-extremal deletion consequence

If a graph has `t` copies of `C_4`, delete one edge from each remaining 4-cycle until none remain. At most `t` edges are deleted. Thus any graph with

\[
m=ex(n,C_4)+1
\]

and `t=o(sqrt(n))` would contain a `C_4`-free subgraph with at least

\[
ex(n,C_4)+1-o(sqrt(n))
\]

edges.

Equivalently, a counterexample to the conjectured `Omega(sqrt(n))` supersaturation must be an `o(sqrt(n))`-edge perturbation of an extremal or near-extremal `C_4`-free graph. The exact codegree identity gives a related statement: small `t` forces the total excess

\[
\sum_{u<v}(c_{uv}-1)_+
\]

to be `o(sqrt(n))`.

Therefore the missing ingredient is an additive `sqrt(n)`-scale stability statement for near-extremal `C_4`-free configurations, not another global pair count.
