## Statement

Can there exist distinct natural numbers `x,y` such that the corresponding three consecutive integers have the same prime supports:

- `primeFactors(x) = primeFactors(y)`,
- `primeFactors(x+1) = primeFactors(y+1)`,
- `primeFactors(x+2) = primeFactors(y+2)`?

Assume `y > x` and write

`D = y - x`.

## Necessary translation divisor

Every prime occurring anywhere in the first triple divides `D`.

If `p | x`, then the support equality at the first coordinate gives `p | y`, hence `p | (y-x)=D`. The same argument applies to `x+1,y+1` and `x+2,y+2`.

Therefore

`rad(x(x+1)(x+2)) | D`,

where `rad(n)` is the product of the distinct prime divisors of `n`.

This is a necessary condition for every solution.

## Excluded family 1: square the middle coordinate

Suppose one tries to preserve the middle prime support by setting

`y+1 = (x+1)^2`.

Then

`y = x(x+2)`.

For `y` to have the same prime support as `x`, every prime factor of `x+2` must already divide `x`. Since

`gcd(x,x+2) | 2`,

this is impossible for odd `x`; for even positive `x`, it forces

`x+2 = 2^m`

for some `m >= 2`.

But then

`y+2 = (x+1)^2 + 1`

`= (2^m-1)^2 + 1`

`= 2(2^(2m-1) - 2^m + 1)`.

The factor in parentheses is odd and greater than `1`, so `y+2` has an odd prime divisor. This contradicts the requirement that `y+2` have the same prime support as `x+2`, which has support `{2}`.

Hence no nontrivial solution lies in this middle-square family.

## Excluded family 2: translate by the whole triple product

Take the strongest obvious multiple of the necessary divisor and set

`D = x(x+1)(x+2)`,  `y=x+D`.

Then direct factorization gives

`y = x(x^2+3x+3)`,

`y+1 = (x+1)^3`,

`y+2 = (x+2)(x^2+x+1)`.

The middle support is preserved automatically by cubing.

If `y` has the same prime support as `x`, every prime factor of `x^2+3x+3` must divide `x`. But

`gcd(x, x^2+3x+3) = gcd(x,3)`.

Since `x^2+3x+3 > 1`, this forces `3 | x`.

Likewise, if `y+2` has the same prime support as `x+2`, every prime factor of `x^2+x+1` must divide `x+2`. Yet

`gcd(x+2, x^2+x+1) = gcd(x+2,3)`.

Since `x^2+x+1 > 1`, this forces `3 | x+2`.

That is impossible. Therefore this entire translation family is excluded.

## What remains

The universal necessary condition

`rad(x(x+1)(x+2)) | (y-x)`

has not yet been converted into a contradiction for an arbitrary admissible difference `D`, nor has a positive witness been constructed. The two exclusion arguments rule out broad natural exponent-preserving families but do not decide the full existence question.
