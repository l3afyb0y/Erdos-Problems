If `A` has divergent reciprocal sum, decompose into dyadic blocks `A_j=A∩[2^j,2^{j+1})`. Then

\[
\sum_{n\in A}\frac1n=\infty
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\sum_j \frac{|A_j|}{2^j}=\infty.
\]

Thus the block densities `δ_j=|A_j|/2^j` have divergent sum, although they may tend to zero arbitrarily slowly. Any quantitative Szemerédi theorem forcing a `k`-term progression in a block whenever `δ_j` exceeds the finite-scale threshold would solve the problem if its threshold were summable against this condition. Present quantitative bounds do not reach that regime. Attempts to combine adjacent blocks or pass to logarithmic density did not remove this gap.

**Final label:** no progress; the discovery pass returned to the known quantitative-Szemerédi barrier.
