# Erdős Problem #10

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN  
**Experiment result:** useful reformulation / no complete progress

## Problem

Is there a fixed `k` such that every integer greater than `1` is a prime plus at most `k` powers of `2`?

## Finite exponent-cover obstruction for a fixed k

To disprove the statement for a particular fixed `k`, it would suffice to construct an arithmetic progression of integers `n` such that for every multiset of at most `k` exponents, the difference

\[
n-\sum_i2^{a_i}
\]

has a forced nontrivial prime divisor.

Choose finitely many odd primes `q`, residues `r_q`, and let `L` be divisible by every `ord_q(2)`. For each `q`, the congruence

\[
\sum_i2^{a_i}\equiv r_q\pmod q
\]

covers a subset of exponent-multisets modulo `L`. If these subsets cover every multiset of size at most `k`, CRT produces an arithmetic progression `n≡r_q (mod q)` on which every candidate prime remainder is composite, apart from finitely many equality exceptions.

This is a finite set-cover problem in exponent space. The two-power case is exactly the special strategy investigated computationally under Problem #9. The discovery pass did not produce certificates for unbounded `k`, nor an invariant showing certificates exist for every fixed `k`.

## Prior art

Covering-congruence constructions are classical in the neighboring prime-plus-powers-of-two problems, and the maintained page records Gallagher's density theorem and the Granville--Soundararajan conjecture. This entry therefore makes no novelty claim for the finite-cover viewpoint.
