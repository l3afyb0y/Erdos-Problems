# Erdős Problem #196

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN  
**Status checked:** 2026-08-30  
**Experiment result:** useful reformulation / anchored multiplicative-order barrier

## Problem

Must every permutation of \(\mathbb N\) contain a monotone 4-term arithmetic progression?

Maintained source: https://www.erdosproblems.com/196

## Discovery record

Let the permutation be \(x_1,x_2,\ldots\), and let \(a=x_1\). Write \(p(n)\) for the position of the value \(n\).

For every positive \(d\) for which the terms are in \(\mathbb N\), the 4-term progression

\[
a,\ a+d,\ a+2d,\ a+3d
\]

is monotone in the permutation exactly when

\[
1=p(a)<p(a+d)<p(a+2d)<p(a+3d).
\]

Thus, with the offset position map

\[
P(d)=p(a+d),
\]

a proof from the first term would follow from forcing

\[
P(d)<P(2d)<P(3d)
\]

for some \(d\).

That apparently simpler multiplicative claim is false as a standalone principle. Consider the acyclic precedence constraints

\[
3d\prec 2d\qquad(d\ge1).
\]

Along every edge the 2-adic valuation increases by one, so there is no directed cycle. Moreover each integer has only finitely many predecessors in the transitive closure (at most its 2-adic valuation). Hence these constraints admit a linear extension of order type \(\omega\), i.e. a permutation of \(\mathbb N\), with

\[
P(3d)<P(2d)\quad\text{for every }d.
\]

Such an order kills every candidate inequality \(P(d)<P(2d)<P(3d)\). Therefore anchoring the desired 4-AP at the first value cannot by itself prove #196; any successful positive argument must couple different starting values, not just multiplicative offsets from \(x_1\).

As a consistency check, the classical three-term argument does not suffer this defect: choosing the first later value larger than \(x_1\) forces the third term of the corresponding 3-AP to occur still later. The fourth term introduces a new relative-order degree of freedom, exactly where the argument breaks.

## Prior-art / status audit

The maintained database remains OPEN and records the classical result of Davis, Entringer, Graham, and Simmons: every permutation of \(\mathbb N\) contains a monotone 3-AP, while a monotone 5-AP can be avoided. Recent public solver records likewise still treat the 4-term case as open.

No novelty claim is made for the anchored reformulation or the precedence obstruction.

## Final classification

**useful reformulation / no complete progress**
