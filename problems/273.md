# Erdős Problem #273

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (2026-08-30)

## Problem

Is there a distinct covering system all of whose moduli are of the form \(p-1\) for primes \(p\ge 5\)?

## Result

**Final label:** known reduction independently rediscovered / construction search inconclusive.

Every permitted modulus is even. Writing a congruence modulo \(2m\) and restricting it to one parity class shows the following exact equivalence: a \((p-1)\)-cover exists if and only if the integers can be covered by two covering systems with disjoint sets of moduli drawn from
\[
\{(p-1)/2:p\ge5\text{ prime}\}.
\]
One half-system covers the even integers after division by two and the other covers the odd integers after translation and division by two. Conversely two such disjoint half-modulus covers lift to a permitted cover of the integers.

For the common period \(55440\), the admissible divisors \(d\mid55440\) with \(d+1\) prime have total reciprocal mass
\[
\sum_d \frac1d = \frac{57861}{55440}>1.
\]
This was the first tested common period in a small divisor-family scan whose available reciprocal mass exceeded 1. A subset of its admissible moduli has reciprocal sum exactly 1, but a distinct exact/disjoint covering is impossible by the Mirsky–Newman–Davenport–Rado exact-cover obstruction; any genuine cover in this family therefore needs overlap.

An exact MILP formulation was built for the residue-class selection problem at period \(55440\), and also after the parity reduction at period \(27720\). The searches hit the local execution time limit and produced neither a construction nor an infeasibility certificate, so they are not mathematical evidence either way.

## Prior-art audit

Post-discovery search found a public 2026 project, `idealombrer/erdos-273-covering-pm1`, which already states and Lean-verifies the same parity equivalence and proves substantially stronger partial obstructions, including that any such covering uses some modulus \(p-1\) with \(p>877\). Therefore no novelty is claimed for the parity reduction or the finite search anatomy recorded here.

The maintained Erdős Problems page still lists #273 as OPEN.

## Conclusion

No complete construction or impossibility proof was obtained.
