# Erdős Problem 36

**Database status checked 2026-08-30:** OPEN.

**Experiment status:** `useful reformulation / elementary identity; no complete solution`

## Problem

Partition `{1,...,2N}` into equal parts `A` and `B`. For an integer shift `x`, let

`C_x = #{(a,b) in A x B : a-b=x}`.

Find the optimal asymptotic constant forcing `max_x C_x >= cN`.

## Exact orientation/boundary identity

For `x>0`, write `f=1_A`. Then

`C_x = sum_{n=1}^{2N-x} f(n+x)(1-f(n))`,

`C_{-x} = sum_{n=1}^{2N-x} f(n)(1-f(n+x))`.

Subtracting cancels the quadratic terms and leaves

`C_x-C_{-x} = sum_{n=1}^{2N-x}(f(n+x)-f(n))`

`= sum_{j=2N-x+1}^{2N} f(j) - sum_{j=1}^{x} f(j)`.

Thus the orientation imbalance at shift `x` depends only on the two boundary windows of length `x`.

Also

`C_x+C_{-x} = sum_{n=1}^{2N-x} |f(n+x)-f(n)|`,

the number of cross-colour pairs at distance `x`. Hence

`max(C_x,C_{-x}) = (T_x + |E_x|)/2`,

where `T_x` is the number of colour changes at distance `x` and `E_x` is the difference between the number of `A`-points in the right and left boundary windows of length `x`.

## Language rendering

At a fixed distance, first ignore direction and count every pair whose two endpoints have opposite colours. Then ask which direction owns those pairs. The directional excess does not depend on the interior at all: after cancellation, only the two boundary strips survive. Any lower-bound argument can therefore be decomposed into two tasks: force many cross-colour pairs at some distance, or force a boundary imbalance at that distance.

## Verification and prior art

The identities above are exact finite telescoping identities and were checked directly from the definitions. They do not approach the current numerical record by themselves. The maintained problem page records substantially stronger bounds, and recent work gives certified lower bounds beyond White's original `0.379005`; therefore no novelty claim is made for this elementary decomposition.
