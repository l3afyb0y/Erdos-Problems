# Erdős Problem #158

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30)

**Final classification:** useful reformulation / exact block-convolution capacity

## Statement

Let `A subset N` be infinite and assume every integer has at most two unordered representations `a+b=n` with `a,b in A`, `a<=b`. Must

\[
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{A(N)}{\sqrt N}=0?
\]

Current status source: https://www.erdosproblems.com/158

## Language-to-structure reduction

The hypothesis says every sum-location has capacity two. Partition the positive integers into equal consecutive blocks

\[
I_i=\{iH+1,\ldots,(i+1)H\},\qquad x_i=|A\cap I_i|.
\]

Fix an integer `q>=0`. Any unordered pair with one point in `I_i` and one in `I_j`, where `i<=j` and `i+j=q`, has sum in the same interval

\[
\{qH+2,\ldots,(q+2)H\},
\]

which contains `2H-1` integers. Each target sum supports at most two unordered representations. Therefore the total number of such pairs is at most `2(2H-1)`.

For `i<j` there are exactly `x_i x_j` cross-pairs. For `i=j` there are `binom(x_i+1,2)` unordered pairs allowing equality. Thus for every `q`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{i<j\\ i+j=q}}x_i x_j
+\mathbf 1_{2\mid q}\binom{x_{q/2}+1}{2}
\le 4H-2.
}
\]

This is an exact discrete convolution-capacity inequality for every equal-block decomposition of every `B_2[2]` set.

## Why this does not solve #158

If one assumes `A(N)>=c sqrt(N)` eventually, the block masses need not be uniform; they can oscillate heavily while still satisfying the displayed convolution inequalities. A contradiction would require turning the family of bounded anti-diagonal convolutions into an unavoidable dip of the cumulative counting function below every positive multiple of `sqrt(N)`.

The direct one-block count only gives the familiar constant-order finite bound, and the two-block product inequalities likewise do not force the desired liminf to vanish. I did not close the missing multiscale step.

## Prior-art / claim audit

The maintained database and recent formalization-oriented summaries still list the `B_2[2]` liminf problem as open. The solved `B_2[1]` (Sidon) case does not extend directly because every sum fiber has an additional unit of capacity.

No complete proof or disproof was found in the narrow current audit. The block-convolution inequality is recorded as a useful exact representation of the constraint, not as a novelty claim.
