# Erdős Problem #173

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30)

**Final classification:** useful reformulation / forced-colour geometric propagation

## Statement

In every 2-colouring of the plane, for all but at most one triangle `T`, is there a monochromatic congruent copy of `T`?

Current status source: https://www.erdosproblems.com/173

## Exact local propagation rule

Fix a triangle with side lengths `(a,b,c)`, using the side of length `c` as base. Suppose a 2-colouring of the plane contains no monochromatic congruent copy of this triangle.

Take any monochromatic pair `x,y` with `|x-y|=c`. The possible third vertices of congruent copies are exactly the two intersections of the circle of radius `a` about one endpoint and the circle of radius `b` about the other (with the radii interchanged when necessary). Every such third vertex must have the opposite colour. Otherwise the pair together with that third point is a monochromatic copy.

Thus avoiding one fixed triangle converts every monochromatic edge of any one side length into forced opposite colours at a finite set of rigid circle-intersection locations. Repeated application turns the problem into a colour-propagation consistency question on the Euclidean graph generated by these rigid motions.

## Discovery attempt

I tried to combine the propagation systems of two distinct forbidden triangle shapes. A contradiction would prove the desired 'at most one' assertion. The obstruction is that two propagation graphs need not share enough forced vertices or return cycles with odd colour parity. Without a rigid relation among their side lengths, the local forcing does not automatically close a contradiction cycle.

The right-angled case is already known by Shader, and recent work on monochromatic 3-term arithmetic progressions in the plane gives related but stronger structure only for special collinear configurations.

## Bottom line

The forced-circle rule is exact but local; I did not find the global parity/return-cycle invariant needed to rule out two simultaneously exceptional noncongruent triangle shapes. Problem #173 remains open.
