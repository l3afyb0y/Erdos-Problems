# Erdős problem 307

**External status at attempt:** OPEN  
**Experiment status:** `known-reduction-rediscovered`  
**Complete solution obtained here:** no

Source statement: `google-deepmind/formal-conjectures`, `ErdosProblems/307.lean`.

## Statement

Do there exist finite sets of primes `P,Q` such that

`1 = (sum_{p in P} 1/p)(sum_{q in Q} 1/q)`?

## Lowest-terms structure

For a finite prime set `S`, define

`D(S) = product_{p in S} p`

and

`N(S) = sum_{p in S} D(S)/p`.

Then

`sum_{p in S} 1/p = N(S)/D(S)`.

This fraction is already in lowest terms. Indeed, for any `r in S`, reducing `N(S)` modulo `r` kills every term except `D(S)/r`, which is nonzero modulo `r`. Hence `r ∤ N(S)` for every prime divisor `r` of `D(S)`, so

`gcd(N(S),D(S)) = 1`.

## Mirror lemma

If positive reduced fractions satisfy

`(a/b)(c/d)=1`,

then `a=d` and `b=c`.

From `ac=bd` and `gcd(a,b)=1`, one gets `a | d`; write `d=at`. Then `c=bt`. Since `gcd(c,d)=1`, necessarily `t=1`.

Applying this to a hypothetical Erdős-307 solution gives

`N(P)=D(Q)` and `N(Q)=D(P)`.

## Disjointness

The prime sets must be disjoint.

If a prime `r` belonged to both `P` and `Q`, then `r | D(Q)=N(P)`. But `r | D(P)` and `gcd(N(P),D(P))=1`, contradiction.

Thus a solution forces two disjoint prime sets whose reciprocal sums `s,t` satisfy `st=1`.

## 59-prime barrier

For positive `s,t` with `st=1`, AM–GM gives

`s+t >= 2`.

Because `P,Q` are disjoint,

`s+t = sum_{r in P union Q} 1/r`.

Among all sets of `k` distinct primes, this reciprocal sum is maximized by taking the first `k` primes. The exact sum of reciprocals of the first `58` primes is

`632922102284802030608735614303171023883353726199065754232874723702048629323675218406677909479222722729203736409`

`/`

`316660540451402206051523961799780143689087330537799173695893334666158783075592938409825136155637880209438531070`

which is approximately `1.998740043147044 < 2`.

Adding the next prime, `277`, raises the sum above `2`.

Therefore any solution must use at least `59` distinct primes in total:

`|P union Q| >= 59`.

## Prior-art status

This barrier is already known and machine checked. The current Formal Conjectures entry records Bonfioli's 2026 Lean proof that any solution uses at least `59` primes total, together with a much stronger prime-product lower bound.

The derivation above was an independent rederivation of the cardinality barrier, not a novel solution.

## Missing step

The main existence question remains open. A complete solution would need either construct prime sets satisfying the reciprocal-product identity or prove that the mirror equations

`N(P)=D(Q)`, `N(Q)=D(P)`

cannot occur for finite prime sets.
