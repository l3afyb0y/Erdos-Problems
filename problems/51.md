# Erdős Problem 51

**Database status checked 2026-08-30:** OPEN.

## Problem

For a totient value `a`, let `n_a` be the least positive integer satisfying

\[
\varphi(n_a)=a.
\]

Are there infinitely many totient values `a` for which

\[
\frac{n_a}{a}\to\infty
\]

along some sequence?

## Exact inverse-totient factorization viewpoint

If

\[
n=\prod_{p\mid n}p^{e_p},
\]

then

\[
\varphi(n)=\prod_{p\mid n}p^{e_p-1}(p-1)
\]

and therefore

\[
\frac{n}{\varphi(n)}=\prod_{p\mid n}\frac{p}{p-1}.
\]

Thus, for fixed totient value `a`, every preimage of `a` corresponds to an exact multiplicative factorization

\[
a=\prod_i p_i^{e_i-1}(p_i-1)
\]

with distinct primes `p_i`, and the ratio attached to that preimage is

\[
\prod_i\frac{p_i}{p_i-1}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\frac{n_a}{a}
=
\min_{\substack{\varphi(n)=a}}
\prod_{p\mid n}\frac{p}{p-1}.
\]

So the problem asks whether one can force **every** inverse-totient factorization of infinitely many values `a` to use prime supports whose reciprocal-prime mass is large enough that the displayed product diverges.

## Local minimality obstruction

Suppose `p` occurs to exponent one in the least preimage `n_a`, so `p\mid n_a` but `p^2\nmid n_a`. If there is an integer `m<p` such that

\[
\varphi(m)=p-1
\]

and

\[
\gcd\!\left(m,\frac{n_a}{p}\right)=1,
\]

then replacing the factor `p` by `m` gives

\[
\varphi\!\left(m\frac{n_a}{p}\right)
=\varphi(m)\varphi\!\left(\frac{n_a}{p}\right)
=(p-1)\varphi\!\left(\frac{n_a}{p}\right)
=a,
\]

while

\[
m\frac{n_a}{p}<n_a,
\]

contradicting minimality. Hence every exponent-one prime in a least inverse-totient preimage must be protected against such a coprime smaller replacement.

## Prior-art audit

A recent public partial-results package dated August 4, 2026 develops substantially stronger structure for this problem. Among other results, it gives an infinite family with exact ratio `n_a/a=2`, derives upper bounds for `n_a/a` in terms of the 2-adic valuation of `a`, and reports certified computation over totient values up to roughly `3.064e10`, with maximum observed ratio about `2.043447`.

Those results dominate the elementary factorization and local-replacement observations above. No novelty claim is made here.
