# Erdős Problem #108

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30 against the maintained Erdős Problems record and current mirrors).

## Statement

For every integers \(r\ge4\) and \(k\ge2\), is there an \(f(k,r)\) such that every graph of chromatic number at least \(f(k,r)\) contains a subgraph of chromatic number at least \(k\) and girth at least \(r\)?

## Attempt

The discovery pass treated the short cycles as the obstruction to be removed while trying to preserve chromatic number. A natural route is to choose edges meeting all cycles of length \(<r\), delete them, and ask whether the residual graph still has chromatic number at least \(k\).

The elementary preservation fact is that deleting one edge lowers chromatic number by at most one. Hence an edge set \(F\) meeting every short cycle would suffice if one could force
\[
|F|\le \chi(G)-k.
\]
The problem is therefore converted into finding a short-cycle transversal whose size is controlled by chromatic excess rather than by the number of short cycles or the number of edges.

This does not close: dense high-chromatic graphs can contain enormous and highly overlapping collections of short cycles, and the obvious greedy or packing bounds control the transversal by edge-scale quantities rather than by \(\chi(G)\). Passing to a critical subgraph gives minimum degree at least \(\chi(G)-1\), but that does not by itself make the short-cycle transversal cheap enough.

## Adversarial check

The edge-deletion Lipschitz fact is correct, but it cannot be reversed: large chromatic number does not imply that all short cycles can be destroyed with few edge deletions. Counting or packing short cycles alone therefore does not yield the required high-girth high-chromatic subgraph.

The maintained record notes the known \(r=4\) case due to Rödl; this attempt produced no extension beyond existing machinery.

## Final classification

**no-progress / short-cycle-transversal barrier.**
