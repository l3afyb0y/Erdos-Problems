# Erdős Problem #101

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30 against the maintained Erdős Problems record and current mirrors).

## Statement

Given \(n\) points in \(\mathbb R^2\), no five of which are collinear, prove that the number \(t_4\) of lines containing four of the points is \(o(n^2)\).

## Candidate partial lemma

For \(n\ge5\),
\[
\boxed{t_4\le \frac{\binom n2-3}{7}}.
\]

This is only a constant-factor quadratic upper bound and does **not** solve the problem.

## Proof

Projectively dualize the point configuration to an arrangement of \(n\) real projective lines. A primal line containing exactly \(r\) input points becomes an intersection point incident to exactly \(r\) dual lines. Because no five primal points are collinear, the dual arrangement has no intersection point of multiplicity at least five.

Let \(t_r\) denote the number of dual intersection points of multiplicity \(r\), for \(r=2,3,4\). Counting unordered pairs of dual lines gives the exact identity
\[
\binom n2=t_2+3t_3+6t_4.
\]

For a real projective line arrangement that is not a pencil, Melchior's inequality gives
\[
t_2\ge 3+\sum_{r\ge4}(r-3)t_r.
\]
Here all \(t_r\) with \(r\ge5\) vanish, so
\[
t_2\ge3+t_4.
\]
Substituting into the pair identity and discarding the nonnegative term \(3t_3\),
\[
\binom n2
=t_2+3t_3+6t_4
\ge (3+t_4)+6t_4
=3+7t_4.
\]
Therefore
\[
t_4\le\frac{\binom n2-3}{7}.
\]

For \(n\ge5\), the no-five-collinear hypothesis excludes the primal configuration in which all \(n\) points are collinear, hence excludes a dual pencil; the Melchior hypothesis is therefore satisfied. Small \(n<5\) are irrelevant to the asymptotic problem and can be checked directly.

## Independent checks

The argument was checked in its two equivalent forms:

1. primal four-point lines \(\leftrightarrow\) four-fold vertices of the dual line arrangement;
2. the exact pair budget \(\binom n2=t_2+3t_3+6t_4\) combined with Melchior's excess-simple-point inequality.

The coefficient \(7\) is forced by one unit of \(t_2\) required per \(t_4\), in addition to the six line pairs already consumed by each four-fold point.

## Adversarial checks

- A four-point primal line corresponds to a four-fold dual vertex, not merely to four concurrent pair intersections counted separately.
- The pair identity counts every pair of distinct dual lines exactly once at their unique projective intersection.
- Melchior's inequality is a theorem for real projective arrangements and would fail as stated over arbitrary fields; the original problem is over \(\mathbb R^2\).
- The non-pencil condition is necessary and follows for \(n\ge5\) from the hypothesis that no five primal points are collinear.
- The result remains \(\Theta(n^2)\), so it does not imply the desired \(o(n^2)\).

## Prior-art audit

The maintained Erdős Problem #101 page (checked 2026-08-30) still labels the problem OPEN and reports no partial or complete solution in its comments. A 2023 paper on four-in-a-line configurations states that, to the authors' knowledge, no progress on the full quadratic-vs-five-collinear question was known, while proving results under an algebraic-curve hypothesis.

Targeted searches for this exact \(1/14\)-scale bound, the formula \(t_4\le(\binom n2-3)/7\), and a Melchior-based treatment of Erdős #101 did not locate an equivalent statement. General arrangement literature contains the elementary pair bound \(t_4\le n(n-1)/12\), but that does not use the no-five hypothesis through Melchior.

Search failure is not proof of novelty. The claim is therefore recorded conservatively as **candidate/new partial lemma — novelty unresolved**.
