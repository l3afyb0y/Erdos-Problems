## Remaining open question

Let `A` be infinite with no distinct `a,b,c in A` satisfying `a | (b+c)` and `b,c>a`. Must

\[
\sum_{n\in A}\frac1n<\infty?
\]

## Local residue orientation

Fix `a in A` and consider the tail `T=A∩(a,∞)`. If two distinct tail elements occupy opposite residue classes `r` and `-r (mod a)`, their sum is divisible by `a`, which is forbidden. Therefore among every non-self-opposite pair of residue classes `{r,-r}`, at most one class can meet `T`. A self-opposite class (`0`, and also `a/2` if `a` is even) contains at most one tail element.

Hence the tail is supported on at most about half of the residue classes modulo every earlier `a`.

The discovery attempt tried to iterate this local halving into a multiplicative/global logarithmic-density loss. The obstruction is dependence: residue orientations imposed by different moduli can align heavily, especially when the moduli share factors, so the local factors cannot simply be multiplied.

## Prior art

A July 2026 working report on this problem explicitly contains the same oriented-tail lemma and develops it further inside CRT constructions. Thus this is a known local reduction, independently rediscovered here, not new progress.
