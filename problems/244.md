# Erdős Problem #244

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30)

## Problem

For fixed real `C>1`, does the set of integers of the form `p + floor(C^k)` have positive density?

## Result

**Final classification:** useful reformulation / known Romanoff-type second-moment route; no new global progress.

Let `a_k=floor(C^k)` and, on a long interval, let `R(n)` count representations `n=p+a_k`. The first moment has the expected constant-order mass because the number of relevant exponents is asymptotic to `log N / log C`, while primes have density `1/log N`.

A standard upper-bound sieve for prime pairs shows that the off-diagonal second moment is controlled by the average, over differences `a_j-a_i`, of the usual prime-pair singular weight

`W(d)=prod_{q|d, q>2} (q-1)/(q-2)`.

Thus a uniform bound on the mean of `W(a_j-a_i)` over exponent pairs would imply a bounded second moment and hence positive density by Cauchy-Schwarz. In language: the only obstruction left by this route is repeated small-prime structure in differences between the exponential shifts.

I did not prove the required uniform mean bound for arbitrary real `C`. The integer-`C` case is Romanoff's theorem, and the maintained page records Ding's result for almost all `C>1`.
