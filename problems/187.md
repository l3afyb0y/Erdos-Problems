# Erdős Problem #187

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30)

**Final classification:** known reduction independently rediscovered / scale-local van der Waerden recursion

## Statement

Find the best function `f(d)` such that every 2-colouring of the integers has, for infinitely many common differences `d`, a monochromatic arithmetic progression of length at least `f(d)`.

Current status source: https://www.erdosproblems.com/187

## Discovery pass

For a colouring `c`, let `L_c(d)` be the largest monochromatic progression length available with common difference `d`.

Van der Waerden gives a scale-local forcing statement. If `W(2,k)` is the usual two-colour van der Waerden number, then for every positive integer `m`, applying van der Waerden to the induced colouring of

`m,2m,...,W(2,k)m`

produces a monochromatic `k`-term progression whose common difference is `mj` with

`1 <= j <= floor((W(2,k)-1)/(k-1))`.

Thus every multiplicative interval of differences

`{m,2m,...,Delta_k m}`

with `Delta_k <= floor((W(2,k)-1)/(k-1))` contains some difference supporting a monochromatic `k`-term progression.

Choosing the next scale strictly larger than the previous witnessed difference produces infinitely many distinct good differences and is the quantitative mechanism behind the classical statement that some admissible lower function tends to infinity.

## Push toward the full problem

To obtain a logarithmic lower bound matching Beck's upper construction in order of magnitude, this scale-local invariant would need an estimate of the shape

`Delta_k <= 2^(O(k))`

along an unbounded sequence of `k`. General van der Waerden bounds are far too large for that. The discovery branch therefore reaches a precise uniform finite-Ramsey bottleneck but does not cross it.

## Prior-art audit

A July 2026 public working report on #187 already develops this exact `Delta_k` scaling lemma, computes sharp values through `k=6`, and isolates `Delta_k <= 2^(O(k))` as the missing route to an `Omega(log d)` lower bound. Thus the present derivation is independently rediscovered, not new.

## Bottom line

No asymptotic improvement was obtained. The problem remains open between the qualitative `f(d)->infinity` lower side and Beck's `(1+o(1)) log_2 d` upper construction.
