# Erdős Problem #143

**Database status at attempt (2026-08-30): OPEN.**

## Problem

Let \(A\subset(1,\infty)\) be countably infinite and satisfy
\[
|kx-y|\ge1
\]
for all distinct \(x,y\in A\) and every integer \(k\ge1\). Must
\[
\sum_{x\in A}\frac1{x\log x}<\infty?
\]
The database also records the weaker harmonic-density question, now known by Koukoulopoulos--Lamzouri--Lichtman to satisfy
\[
\sum_{\substack{x<n\\x\in A}}\frac1x=o(\log n).
\]

Current database record: https://www.erdosproblems.com/143

## Exact multiplicative-shadow representation

For each fixed \(x\in A\), every other point of \(A\) must avoid the full family of open intervals
\[
(kx-1,kx+1),\qquad k=1,2,3,\ldots.
\]
Equivalently, for distinct \(x,y\in A\),
\[
\operatorname{dist}(y/x,\mathbb Z_{>0})\ge \frac1x.
\]
Thus each element casts a sequence of width-\(2\) forbidden shadows around all of its positive integer multiples. The unresolved convergence question can be viewed as asking whether the cumulative overlap geometry of these multiplicative shadows forces more than the already-known \(o(\log n)\) harmonic sparsity.

A direct measure count of the shadows from a fixed \(x\) across an interval of length comparable to \(N\) gives total raw length of order \(N/x\), suggesting a connection with the reciprocal sum. However, shadows generated by different \(x\)'s can overlap heavily, and the defining hypothesis gives no immediate uniform bound on this overlap multiplicity. That overlap is exactly where the elementary covering argument fails.

## Adversarial check

One cannot sum the lengths \(2N/x\) over \(x\in A\) and infer comparable union measure: near-coincidences \(kx\approx \ell z\) between multiples of two different elements are not excluded by the original condition. The hypothesis only separates actual elements of \(A\) from multiples of other actual elements; it does not separate the entire two multiplier lattices from each other.

## Prior-art / status audit

The maintained page remains open and records the 2025 Koukoulopoulos--Lamzouri--Lichtman partial theorem above. Searches through August 2026 found no complete proof of the reciprocal-logarithmic convergence. The shadow reformulation is elementary and is not claimed as new.

**Final classification:** `useful reformulation / multiplicative-shadow overlap barrier; no new quantitative progress`.
