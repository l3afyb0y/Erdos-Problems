# Erdős Problem 66

**Database status checked 2026-08-30:** OPEN.

## Problem

Does there exist `A subset N` and a nonzero constant `c` such that, for the ordered representation function

\[
r_A(n)=(1_A*1_A)(n),
\]

we have

\[
\frac{r_A(n)}{\log n}\to c?
\]

Since `r_A(n)>=0`, any such nonzero limit must have `c>0`.

## Necessary counting-function asymptotic

Let

\[
F(x)=\sum_{a\in A}x^a,
\qquad
A(N)=|A\cap[1,N]|.
\]

If

\[
r_A(n)\sim c\log n,
\]

then

\[
F(x)^2=\sum_{n\ge0}r_A(n)x^n
\sim \frac{c}{1-x}\log\frac1{1-x}
\qquad (x\to1^-).
\]

Taking the positive square root gives

\[
F(x)\sim \sqrt c\,(1-x)^{-1/2}
\left(\log\frac1{1-x}\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Karamata's Tauberian theorem for power series with nonnegative coefficients therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
A(N)\sim \frac{2\sqrt c}{\sqrt\pi}\sqrt{N\log N}.
}
\]

Thus a hypothetical pointwise logarithmic representation law forces not merely the `sqrt(N log N)` scale but an exact leading constant.

## Exact parity consequence

For ordered representations, all off-diagonal representations of `2m` occur in reversed pairs, while the diagonal representation `(m,m)` occurs exactly when `m in A`. Therefore

\[
r_A(2m)\equiv1_A(m)\pmod2.
\]

Combining this exact parity identity with the counting asymptotic gives the necessary law

\[
\boxed{
|\{m\le N:r_A(2m)\text{ is odd}\}|
\sim \frac{2\sqrt c}{\sqrt\pi}\sqrt{N\log N}.
}
\]

So any construction with `r_A(n)/log n -> c` must encode its membership set exactly in the sparse parity pattern of the even representation values, on this prescribed asymptotic scale.

## Why this does not settle the problem

The condition `r_A(n)=c log n+o(log n)` allows errors much larger than the `sqrt(log n)` scale excluded by the Erdős--Sárközy and Horváth results. The parity condition also does not contradict the limit: an integer sequence can remain asymptotic to `c log n` while having a sparse prescribed parity pattern.

No construction or contradiction closing that remaining freedom was obtained.

## Prior-art audit

The parity identity is classical; literature on representation functions explicitly notes that the parities of `r_A(2n)` determine `A`. The Tauberian asymptotic is a standard consequence of the generating-function hypothesis. No novelty claim is made for either ingredient or their combination here.

## Classification

**useful reformulation / necessary asymptotic** — no complete solution.
