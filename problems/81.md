# Erdős Problem 81

**Database status checked 2026-08-30:** OPEN.

**Experiment status:** `useful reformulation / no new asymptotic progress`

## Problem

Can the edges of every chordal graph on `n` vertices be partitioned into

`n^2/6 + O(n)`

cliques?

## Fixed-partition simplicial extension lemma

Let `v` be a simplicial vertex of a chordal graph `G`, with clique neighborhood `C=N(v)`, and let `P` be an edge-clique partition of `G-v`.

Call a block `K in P` **eligible** if its vertex set is contained in `C`. Choose a family `F` of eligible blocks whose vertex sets are pairwise disjoint. Each `K in F` can be replaced by `K union {v}` without changing the number of blocks. This adds all edges from `v` to vertices of `K` while preserving every old edge exactly once.

After doing this, every vertex of `C` not covered by the selected blocks can be joined to `v` by its own two-vertex clique. Consequently, if `u(F)` is the number of vertices of `C` covered by `F`, then `P` extends to a clique partition of `G` with

`|P| + |C| - u(F)`

blocks.

Optimizing over such families gives the exact bound

`cp(G) <= |P| + |C| - nu_P(C)`,

where `nu_P(C)` is the maximum number of vertices of `C` covered by a vertex-disjoint family of partition blocks lying wholly inside `C`.

## Interpretation

The local cost of adding a simplicial vertex is therefore controlled not just by its degree but by how much of its clique neighborhood remains packaged into reusable internal clique blocks in the existing partition.

This explains the obstruction in the standard split-graph family: a large clique block can be extended through one independent simplicial vertex, but after that extension the block is no longer contained wholly in the common neighborhood of the next independent vertex. The same internal clique edges cannot be consumed repeatedly for free.

## Why this did not close the problem

A chordal perfect-elimination ordering gives simplicial vertices one at a time, but the quantity `nu_P(C)` depends on the entire evolving clique partition, not just the chordal graph. The argument therefore becomes an online resource-allocation problem for separator-clique edges. No invariant was found that forces the total accumulated cost down to `n^2/6+O(n)`.

The existing literature already gives a strictly-below-`n^2/4` bound for all chordal graphs and `3n^2/16+O(n)` for split graphs. June 2026 public notes contain further partial/conditional improvements, but no complete solution to the `1/6` conjecture.

## Status

The fixed-partition extension lemma is elementary bookkeeping and is recorded only as a useful exact formulation. No novelty or solution claim is made.
