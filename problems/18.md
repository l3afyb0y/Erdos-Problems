# Erdős Problem #18

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN

**Experiment status:** no progress / computational reconnaissance

## Statement

For a practical number `m`, let `h(m)` be the least integer such that every integer below `m` is a sum of at most `h(m)` distinct divisors of `m`. Are there infinitely many practical `m` with `h(m) < (log log m)^{O(1)}`? Related factorial bounds are also open.

## Reconnaissance

A direct dynamic-programming computation of exact `h(m)` for small practical numbers reproduces the expected slow growth on highly divisible examples. For example:

- `h(60)=4`
- `h(420)=5`
- `h(840)=5`
- `h(2520)=5`
- `h(27720)=6`

The least-common-multiple sequence `L_k=lcm(1,...,k)` is therefore an attractive test family, since every integer up to `k` is automatically a divisor of `L_k` and the divisor set becomes increasingly redundant.

However, no argument was found that bounds `h(L_k)` by a fixed power of `log k` (which would be strong enough to resolve the first conjecture, since `log L_k` is of order `k`). The obvious greedy, mixed-radix, and divisor-density decompositions lose too much and do not beat known scales.

## Prior-art audit

The Erdős Problems database still lists #18 as OPEN and records Vose's theorem giving infinitely many practical `m` with `h(m) << (log m)^{1/2}`. No partial result from this run improves that bound.
