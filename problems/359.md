# Erdős Problem #359

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30)

## Problem

Let `a_1<a_2<...`, with `a_1=n`, and let each `a_{k+1}` be the least positive integer larger than `a_k` which is not a sum of consecutive earlier terms. What can be said about its density? In particular for `n=1`, must `a_k/k -> infinity` and `a_k/k^(1+c) -> 0` for every `c>0`?

## Result

**Final classification:** useful elementary reformulation; no progress on the stated asymptotic.

Let `S_0=0` and `S_j=a_1+...+a_j`. A sum of consecutive earlier terms is exactly a positive difference `S_j-S_i`. Therefore

`a_{k+1} = mex( Delta^+( {S_0,...,S_k} ) )`,

where `mex` means the least positive missing integer and `Delta^+` is the set of positive pairwise differences.

Consequently all integers `1,...,a_{k+1}-1` occur among the at most `k(k+1)/2` positive differences of `k+1` prefix sums. Hence the unconditional counting bound

`a_{k+1} <= k(k+1)/2 + 1`.

This is far too weak for the requested `a_k = k^(1+o(1))`-type upper behaviour, but it identifies the process exactly as a greedy least-missing-distance construction on its prefix-sum ruler.

The attempted next step was to exploit the special fact that adding `S_k` contributes a nested family of suffix-sum differences. I did not obtain enough forced collisions among those differences to improve the quadratic bound to a subquadratic estimate relevant to the conjecture.
