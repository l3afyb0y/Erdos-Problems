# Erdős Problem #686

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (2026-08-30)

## Problem

Can every integer \(N\ge2\) be represented as
\[
N=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^k(m+i)}{\prod_{i=1}^k(n+i)}
\]
for some \(k\ge2\) and \(m\ge n+k\)?

## Result

**Final label:** known reduction independently rediscovered / no new progress on the square case.

For \(k=2\), setting
\[
x=2m+3,\qquad y=2n+3
\]
turns the equation into
\[
x^2-Ny^2=1-N.
\]
If \(N\) is nonsquare, start from the solution \(1+\sqrt N\) of norm \(1-N\) and multiply by powers of a positive Pell unit of norm 1. Passing to a suitable positive power of the unit makes the multiplier congruent to 1 modulo 2, so infinitely many resulting coefficient pairs \((x,y)\) are odd. Since \(x/y\to\sqrt N>1\), eventually \(x-y\ge4\), giving \(m\ge n+2\). Thus every nonsquare \(N\) is representable already with \(k=2\).

For a square \(N=s^2\), the same equation factors as
\[
(x-sy)(x+sy)=1-s^2,
\]
so the \(k=2\) possibilities are finite and the Pell orbit disappears. Computation during the attempt confirmed that some squares have \(k=2\) or \(k=3\) representations while many small squares do not in the searched range; no uniform construction for all squares emerged.

## Prior-art audit

Post-discovery inspection of the public #686 discussion found that the nonsquare Pell reduction and the resulting reduction of the problem to square \(N\) were already known there by March 2026. The discussion also contains additional work on the square case. Therefore this attempt makes no novelty claim.

The maintained Erdős Problems page still lists #686 as OPEN.

## Conclusion

The problem remains reduced to its square-input case; no complete solution was obtained.
