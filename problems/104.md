# Erdős Problem #104

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30 against the maintained Erdős Problems record and current prize/status mirrors).

## Statement

Given \(n\) points in the plane, show that the number \(M\) of unit circles containing at least three of the points is \(o(n^2)\).

## Exact incidence reformulation

Associate to every counted unit circle its center \(c\). Let \(C\) be this set of centers and \(P\) the original \(n\)-point set. Join \(p\in P\) to \(c\in C\) when \(|p-c|=1\). Every \(c\in C\) has degree at least three, so if \(I\) is the number of incidences,
\[
3M\le I.
\]

For \(p\in P\), let \(r_p\) be the number of counted unit circles through \(p\). Every such circle contains at least two further points of \(P\). For any second point \(q\ne p\), there are at most two unit circles through \(p,q\), and none unless \(|p-q|\le2\). Counting the two required companions per circle gives
\[
2r_p\le 2d_p,
\]
where \(d_p\) is the number of other points of \(P\) within distance at most \(2\). Hence \(r_p\le d_p\). Summing,
\[
I=\sum_p r_p\le\sum_p d_p=2E_{\le2},
\]
where \(E_{\le2}\) is the number of pairs of points at distance at most \(2\). Thus
\[
M\le \frac23 E_{\le2}\le \frac{n(n-1)}3.
\]

Equivalently, choosing three witnesses on each counted circle produces a 3-uniform hypergraph on \(P\) with pair-codegree at most two; the same pair budget gives the same \(n(n-1)/3\) upper bound.

## Discovery outcome

The reformulation makes the missing ingredient explicit: bounded pair-codegree alone permits quadratic size, so the desired \(o(n^2)\) must exploit geometric compatibility among many unit-circle triples, not merely the fact that two points determine at most two unit circles.

## Adversarial check

The inequality above is exact but no stronger than the classical trivial bound. Reusing the same pair in two different unit circles is genuinely possible, so replacing codegree two by one would be false. The number of short pairs \(E_{\le2}\) can itself be quadratic, so the metric cutoff does not by itself yield \(o(n^2)\).

## Prior-art note

The maintained problem record already gives the trivial upper bound \(n(n-1)/3\) and an Elekes lower construction of order \(n^{3/2}\). No improvement to either asymptotic frontier was obtained here.

## Final classification

**useful reformulation / known pair-incidence barrier.**
