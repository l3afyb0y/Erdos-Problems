# Erdős Problem #261

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30)

**Final classification:** useful exact reformulation / candidate prefix-closure
lemma - novelty unresolved / extended finite verification

## Statement

Let `w(a) = a / 2^a`.

For which positive integers `n` can `w(n)` be written as a sum of at least two
distinct later terms `w(a)`? Is this possible for every positive `n`? Is there
a rational number having continuum-many representations as a sum of distinct
terms `w(a)`?

Current status source: [Erdős Problems #261](https://www.erdosproblems.com/261)

## Exact offset grammar

**Let** `D` be a finite set of distinct positive offsets.

**Let** `S` be the sum of `1 / 2^d` over `d` in `D`.

**Let** `T` be the sum of `d / 2^d` over `d` in `D`.

**Then** the later indices `n+d`, with `d` in `D`, represent `w(n)` exactly if

`n * (1-S) = T`.

**Because** `w(a)` is strictly decreasing after its first two values, positivity
forces every term in a nontrivial representation of `w(n)` to have index larger
than `n`. After writing each index as `n+d` and multiplying the desired identity
by `2^n`, the right side becomes `n*S+T`. Therefore the desired identity is
equivalent to `n=n*S+T`, which is the displayed criterion.

**Conversely**, if a finite offset set satisfies the criterion, reversing these
steps gives the required representation. Thus this is an equivalence, not only
a necessary condition.

## Known consecutive family, independently recovered

**Let** `D={1,...,m}`.

**Then** `1-S=1/2^m` and `T=2-(m+2)/2^m`.

**Therefore** the represented target is

`n = 2^(m+1)-m-2`.

This is the Borwein-Loring family already recorded in the literature. It proves
that infinitely many targets work, but it does not move the unresolved
"for every positive n" quantifier.

## Candidate prefix-closure lemma

**Suppose** `D` represents `n`, and let `q=1-S`.

**Write** `q=A/2^M` in lowest terms. Then `A` is odd.

**Choose** a positive integer `h` for which `A` divides `2^h-1`.

**Form** a new offset set by taking the full prefix `{1,...,h}` and then placing
a shifted copy of `D` after it:

`D_h = {1,...,h} union {h+d : d in D}`.

**Then** `D_h` represents the integer

`n_h = n-h + (2^(h+1)-2)/q`.

### Proof

**Let** `S_h` and `T_h` be the two offset sums for `D_h`.

**Because** the new set consists of a full prefix followed by a shifted copy,

`1-S_h = q/2^h`.

**Because** shifting an old offset `d` to `h+d` contributes both the old first
moment and `h` times the old mass,

`T_h = 2 - (2+(h-n)*q)/2^h`.

**Therefore** `T_h / (1-S_h)` is exactly the displayed value `n_h`.

**Because** `q=A/2^M`, the only possible divisibility obstruction is `A`.
The choice of `h` removes it: `A` divides `2^h-1`, hence also
`2^(h+1)-2`. Thus `n_h` is an integer.

**Finally**, powers of two are periodic modulo the odd number `A`, so infinitely
many positive `h` satisfy the condition. Along those values, `n_h` tends to
infinity. Hence every finite solution generates an explicit infinite family
of further finite solutions.

### Nonconsecutive example

The offsets `{2,3,4}` represent target `n=2`. Here `q=9/16`, and the order of
`2` modulo `9` is `6`. Taking `h=6` gives

`D_6 = {1,2,3,4,5,6,8,9,10}`

and

`n_6 = 220`.

The exact checker confirms that these offsets represent `w(220)`.

This closure lemma is stronger than one isolated construction, but it still does
not prove that every target works and does not address the rational
continuum-representation question.

## Greedy recurrence

**Let** `a` be the current candidate index.

**Let** `r/2^a` be the part of `w(n)` not yet represented.

**Initially**, `a=n+1` and `r=2*n`.

**If** `r<a`, then `w(a)` is too large to use, so omit it and replace `r` by
`2*r`.

**If** `r>=a`, then include `w(a)` and replace `r` by `2*(r-a)`.

**Because** `0<=r<2*a` is preserved, these cases combine into the exact integer
recurrence

`r <- 2*(r mod a)`, followed by `a <- a+1`.

**If** this recurrence reaches zero, then the selected terms give a finite exact
representation. For `n>=2`, the first selected term cannot exhaust the target,
so the representation has at least two terms.

The boundary target `n=1` needs a separate non-greedy witness:

`w(1) = w(3)+w(6)+w(8)`.

## Reproduction and adversarial checks

Two separate checkers were written.

- `verification/261_offset_prefix.py` uses exact `Fraction` arithmetic. It
  checks the consecutive construction for lengths `2` through `20`, the target
  `1` boundary witness, the nonconsecutive `2 -> 220` example, and `58` prefix
  transformations obtained by exhaustively enumerating all representable
  subsets of `{1,...,12}`.
- `verification/261_greedy_recurrence.rs` implements only the integer recurrence.
  It reproduces the published peak trajectories for targets `56`, `3113`,
  `3817`, and `5588`, has an explicit per-target stopping limit, and reports a
  limit hit as unresolved rather than as a counterexample.

The compiled recurrence checker terminated for every target from `2` through
`100000`, extending the published finite range `n<=10000`. The longest
trajectory in that range started at target `88225`, took `571263` recurrence
steps, selected `285549` terms, and stopped just after its last selected index
`659488`.

This remains finite evidence. It cannot discharge the universal quantifier.

The boundary audit also caught and corrected an initial verifier mistake: the
greedy path for target `1` is the inadmissible one-term identity `w(1)=w(2)`.
The final checker therefore sweeps only targets starting at `2` and verifies the
three-term target `1` witness separately.

## Prior-art audit

Borwein and Loring proved the consecutive family above and developed the greedy
recurrence. They also constructed a dense set of irrational numbers having
uncountably many representations; their construction does not supply the
rational number asked for here.

Tengely, Ulas, and Zygadlo proved further finite-solution structure and checked
the greedy construction through target `10000`. The current Erdős Problems page
still labels the full problem open and lists no complete proof claim.

Targeted searches for the prefix-closure formula and for later work resolving the
all-target or rational-continuum questions did not locate an equivalent result.
Search failure is not evidence of novelty, so the prefix lemma remains
**novelty unresolved**.

## Bottom line

Problem #261 remains open. The attempt produced an exact offset equivalence, an
explicit prefix-closure construction with independent exact checks, and a larger
finite greedy verification. It did not prove the all-target statement or produce
a rational number with continuum-many representations.
