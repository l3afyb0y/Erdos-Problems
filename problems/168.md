# Erdős Problem #168

**Database status at attempt:** OPEN (checked 2026-08-30)

**Final classification:** known reduction independently rediscovered / certified-tail route only

## Statement

Let `F(N)` be the largest size of a subset of `{1,...,N}` containing no `{n,2n,3n}`. Determine `lim F(N)/N` and whether it is irrational.

Current status source: https://www.erdosproblems.com/168

## Discovery result

Write every integer uniquely as `r 2^a 3^b` with `gcd(r,6)=1`. Inside one fixed `r`-fiber the forbidden triple is exactly the lattice corner

`(a,b), (a+1,b), (a,b+1)`.

Thus the global problem is a weighted direct sum of one finite corner-free optimization problem on initial regions of the nonnegative exponent lattice. This recovers the Graham–Spencer–Witsenhausen reduction: if `d_1<d_2<...` are the 3-smooth numbers and `f(k)` is the corner-free optimum on the first `k` exponent points, then the limiting density is

`(1/3) sum_{k: f(k)>f(k-1)} 1/d_k`.

A finite prefix gives an automatically rigorous enclosure once its optimum values are certified: the omitted contribution is nonnegative and at most one third of the reciprocal tail of all omitted 3-smooth numbers. Because the latter is an explicitly summable geometric lattice tail, certified finite optimization converts directly into a certified interval for the limiting density.

## Adversarial / prior-art audit

This decomposition is already the standard GSW architecture recorded on the live page. A recent August 2026 computation extends the finite frontier to `f(5020)=3347`, but does not resolve the density exactly or its irrationality. No new infinite invariant was found in this run.

## Bottom line

The run recovered a clean proof-carrying numerical-enclosure route but no new asymptotic or arithmetic information about the limiting constant. Problem #168 remains open.
