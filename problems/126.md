# Erdős Problem #126

**Database status at attempt (2026-08-30): OPEN.**

## Problem

For an \(n\)-element set \(A\subset\mathbb N\), let the relevant prime set be the distinct primes dividing at least one sum \(a+b\) with distinct \(a,b\in A\). If \(f(n)\) is the minimum possible size of this prime set over all such \(A\), must
\[
\frac{f(n)}{\log n}\to\infty?
\]

Current database record: https://www.erdosproblems.com/126

## Attempt and strongest valid reduction

Suppose all pairwise sums from \(A\) are composed of a fixed set \(S\) of \(m\) primes. Then every \(a+b\), \(a\ne b\), is an \(S\)-unit in the positive integers. For four distinct elements \(a,b,c,d\in A\), the six pair sums are therefore \(S\)-units and in particular satisfy the exact additive relation
\[
(a+b)+(c+d)=(a+c)+(b+d).
\]
Because all four displayed terms are positive, this is a nondegenerate four-term \(S\)-unit equation after projectivising by any one term. Thus a counterexample with only \(m\) relevant primes gives a large clique in the graph whose vertices are positive integers and whose edges join pairs having \(S\)-unit sum, together with a very dense family of nondegenerate projective \(S\)-unit relations.

This does recover the natural Diophantine bottleneck: quantitative \(S\)-unit bounds can force \(m\gg\log n\), but the desired conclusion requires a genuinely stronger dependence than an exponential bound for the size of such a clique. I did not obtain such a strengthening.

The classical Erdős--Turán theorem already gives the logarithmic lower bound (indeed, their old square-class argument gives an exponential upper bound on the size of a set whose pair sums use only \(m\) primes). A 2026 paper of Füredi and Gyarmati also explicitly describes the Erdős--Turán theorem in this form. No step in this attempt improves that frontier.

## Adversarial check

The relation above alone does not inject four-tuples of \(A\) into distinct projective \(S\)-unit solutions: many different quadruples can share projective data up to scaling. Treating the number of quadruples as though it directly bounded the number of projective \(S\)-unit solutions would therefore be invalid. This is exactly where the tempting counting route loses enough information to remain at logarithmic scale.
