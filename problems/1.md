# Erdős Problem 1

**Database status checked 2026-08-30:** OPEN.

**Experiment classification:** known reduction independently rediscovered / no new progress.

## Problem

If `A subset {1,...,N}`, `|A|=n`, and all `2^n` subset sums are distinct, must `N >> 2^n`?

## Attempt

Let `X` be the sum of a uniformly random subset of `A`. Distinct subset sums make `X` uniform on `2^n` values, so its entropy is exactly `n log 2`. On the other hand,

`Var(X) = (1/4) sum_{a in A} a^2 <= nN^2/4`.

The standard entropy/variance bound for an integer-valued random variable therefore gives only

`N >> 2^n/sqrt(n)`.

The same obstruction appears if the subset sums are grouped by their cardinality: the middle layer has `binom(n,floor(n/2))` distinct sums and immediately gives the sharp known central-binomial-scale lower bound.

A recursive split according to a largest element was also tested. Unless the largest element separates the two translated subset-sum clouds, the clouds must interlace without colliding; controlling that interlacing again reduces to anti-concentration at the `sqrt(n)` scale. No invariant was found that removes the remaining square-root loss.

## Prior-art audit

The maintained page records the exact lower bound `N >= binom(n,floor(n/2))` due to Dubroff, Fox, and Xu (also obtained at the same leading constant by earlier work of Elkies and Gleason). The entropy and middle-layer routes above do not improve it.

No new partial result is claimed.
